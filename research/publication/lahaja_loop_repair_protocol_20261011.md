# Frozen Lahaja repetition-repair test

**Frozen on 11 October 2026 before examining the Whisper-small held-out output.** This is a narrow inference-time control, not a training claim.

Whisper-small greedy decoding on 104 first-shard development clips produced sustained adjacent single-word loops. Twenty-five outputs contained a run of at least 20 identical words; the other development outputs had no adjacent run longer than four. We fixed a reference-free rule: when a hypothesis contains **five or more adjacent copies of the same Unicode-normalized word, retain one copy**. The implementation is [`loop_repair.py`](../../src/publication_study/loop_repair.py), SHA-256 `4a89775ecae07364dd69a661e1ac612a3ffd10c7991f76305b45ae48c8873867`. At inference it reads only the model's prediction. It does not read the transcript, speaker metadata, or evaluation result.

On the development speakers, the rule changed 25 of 104 clips, helped all 25, harmed none, and reduced pooled WER from **120.63% to 73.19%** (2,403 to 1,458 errors over 1,992 reference words). The paired 26-speaker bootstrap difference was **−47.44 points [−63.53, −33.27]**. Five-beam decoding of the same model scored **79.47%** and still had 12 utterances above 100% WER and 18 generation-cap hits. These are development diagnostics; the threshold and decoder choice were selected after inspecting this sample. Full paired receipts are [repair](../../results/night1/lahaja_loop_repair_dev_20261011.json) and [beam](../../results/night1/lahaja_small_decoder_dev_20261011.json).

The confirmatory sample is the previously frozen **405 clips from 106 other speakers**, with no overlap with the 26 development speakers and selection SHA-256 `6134e2a9eabd65f590bf4f7f7829626e520379a66e058d9dd51ce4acf36c4060`. The pinned model is `openai/whisper-small@973afd24965f72e36ca33b3055d56a652f456b4d`, greedy, Hindi transcription, MPS float16, 256 generated-token cap. The primary comparison is raw versus repaired pooled word error rate on *identical clips*, with a 4,000-resample paired speaker-bootstrap interval. We also report how many predictions change, improve, or worsen and how many utterances exceed 100% WER. All clips stay in the denominator. The scorer lowercases NFC text and retains Unicode letters, marks, and numbers; it does not equate spoken number words with written digits. We will not adjust the rule after seeing the held-out predictions.

The result would establish whether this particular failure mode generalizes to unseen Lahaja speakers. It would not establish regional fairness, semantic correctness of numbers or terms, or publication-level novelty by itself. Those claims require listening review, task-specific labels, independent corpora, and a comparison with strong open models. Raw gated Lahaja audio and transcripts remain local; only aggregate receipts are shared.
