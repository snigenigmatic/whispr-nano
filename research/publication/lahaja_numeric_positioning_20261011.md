# What Lahaja can and cannot contribute to our paper

**11 October 2026 · C. Kaustubh's capstone**

The [Lahaja benchmark paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2408.11440) already introduced multi-accent Hindi evaluation with 132 speakers, read and extempore speech, digital interactions containing numbers and alphanumeric codes, and a named-entity “task of five.” It reported a full-set Whisper V3 WER of 32.4% and an IndicASR variant at 14.3%, plus high error on named-entity and specialist-domain categories. Our new first-shard Whisper Turbo result is a useful, provenance-checked baseline; it is not directly comparable with those published figures because the model, sample, and normalization differ. Rediscovering that accents and terms are hard would add no research contribution.

The released text fields require care. A metadata scan of all 6,152 rows found brackets in 3,960 `normalized` strings, and `verbatim` differs from the cleaned `text` field in 3,175 rows. Some brackets are inserted glosses: for example, `normalized` can insert `[Media]` after the spoken Hindi word `मीडिया`. Our pilot scores `verbatim` as the literal reference and keeps number words distinct from digit strings. This is transparent but intentionally different from any publication score whose text field or normalization policy is not matched exactly.

Numeral formatting is also established prior art. [The Hidden Cost of Digits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2609.21084) shows that number normalization alone can shift ASR WER by more than two points on Polish speech. [mu-bench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2609.32082) evaluates names, addresses and confirmation codes using a human-calibrated utterance error measure; its authors report Cohen's κ of 0.78 for that measure on 1,847 human-rated transcripts. Thus a Hindi number normalizer or a new headline “number-aware WER” would need a much sharper empirical contribution than a simple scoring change. Our raw Lahaja scorer deliberately leaves spoken number words and written digits distinct so this ambiguity remains visible rather than silently credited.

The viable experiment is a **small open-ASR intervention**, not another survey: develop a fixed, cheap decoder or correction policy using only the 26 first-shard Lahaja speakers and the 30-clip bilingual review packet; then evaluate without tuning on the 106 disjoint speakers selected from the other three shards. Compare Whisper-small greedy, a bounded beam/fallback policy, and a stronger open-model reference on the same IDs. Report pooled and speaker-clustered WER, numeric-value exact match and entity/term recall **alongside false insertions on absent terms**, plus latency and memory. A publication claim exists only if the small-model policy improves the held-out task measure without unacceptable WER or false-positive regressions. The present first-shard Turbo score and ongoing held-out extension establish evaluation infrastructure, not that claim.
