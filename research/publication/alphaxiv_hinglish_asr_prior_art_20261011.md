# Hindi–English ASR, script-aware scoring, contextual terms, and decoder failures: primary-source map

**Checked:** 11 October 2026. **Question:** what has already been done near a Hindi–English/Indian-English ASR study that separates recognition, writing-system choice, term recognition, and decoder failures? **Discovery route:** searched alphaXiv for Hindi–English code switching, script-tolerant WER, Indic context evaluation, language adherence, and Whisper repetition. Example alphaXiv entry points: [MUCS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2104.00235), [CS-FLEURS](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2509.14161), [IndicContextEval](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.19157), and [Vimarsha](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.24199). Every substantive claim below was then checked against the linked original paper, its official proceedings record, author repository, or dataset card. An alphaXiv overview is a discovery aid, not evidence. Sources are scoped to what they actually tested.

## Findings that set the novelty boundary

1. **Hindi–English ASR and script-tolerant scoring are established.** Direct Hindi–English ASR studies date at least to 2018–19. The 2021 MUCS challenge released Hindi–English lecture speech and explicitly scored English words in Latin or Devanagari with T-WER. Earlier toWER and poWER work addressed transliteration and pronunciation equivalence. A claim to be the first Hinglish recognizer or first script-aware WER would be wrong. [Srivastava & Sitaram, 2018](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/homophone-merging-interspeech2018-cameraready.pdf); [Emond et al., 2018](https://research.google/pubs/transliteration-based-approaches-to-improve-code-switched-speech-recognition-performance/); [Diwan et al., 2021, §§2.2.2, 3.2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.00235).
2. **Variant-aware Indian-language evaluation is already a major research line.** OIWER represents accepted orthographic forms as ordered variation groups, and later Voice of India and Vimarsha use multiple valid transcripts/lattices in large Indian-language benchmarks. Vimarsha evaluates ten systems on 99.4 hours spanning 22 scheduled languages and combines controlled and difficult in-the-wild audio. Its public repository presently has evaluation and lattice utilities but says the benchmark data will be released separately. A new normalizer or simple alternative-reference score is not a standalone novelty claim. [OIWER, 2026, §§4–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00941); [Voice of India, 2026, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19151); [Vimarsha, 2026, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24199); [author repository](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/Vimarsha).
3. **Context prompts and named-entity metrics are established, including Indic evaluation.** IndicContextEval tests seven prompt conditions, English- versus native-script entity lists, unrelated-domain distractors, WER, and named-entity error rate on 55.93 hours/16,884 utterances in eight Indian languages. Its instruction makes English words native-script transliterations, so it is not a mixed-script Hinglish benchmark. CB-Whisper and RECAST already select likely entities before Whisper prompting; PROCTER and PARCO inject pronunciation or phoneme information. A mere list-versus-prompt ablation or “pronunciation-aware entity biasing” would be weak without a sharper setting and stronger baselines. [IndicContextEval, 2026, §§3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19157); [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/IndicContextEval); [CB-Whisper, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.262/); [RECAST, 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.203/); [PROCTER, 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17131); [PARCO, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04357).
4. **Language controls and hallucination/repetition are not unexplored.** Whisper code-switch prompting with two language tokens was tested on Mandarin–English, and a 2026 study tested correct, absent, mixed, and distractor language hints on monolingual and synthetic code-switched speech, including Hindi–English. Whisper's own transcriber documents compression/log-probability fallback and previous-text conditioning; separate human-annotated studies measure unsupported speech and long-form repetition. The open question is the *causal behavior of a specified recipe on this specific corpus*, not whether these failure modes exist. [Peng et al., 2023, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.11095); [Kim et al., 2026, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17281); [Whisper transcriber](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/transcribe.py); [Koenecke et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08021); [Ahn et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06193).

### A precise scoring fact, and the denominator caveat

Let \(r=(r_1,\ldots,r_N)\) be the original reference, \(h\) a hypothesis, \(d\) unit-cost word edit distance, and \(\mathcal A(r)\) a *human-approved* set of equivalent references containing \(r\). Define a **fixed-denominator sensitivity score** \(W_{\mathcal A}(h)=\min_{a\in\mathcal A(r)}d(a,h)/N\), and original \(W_r(h)=d(r,h)/N\). Since \(r\in\mathcal A(r)\), \(W_{\mathcal A}(h)\le W_r(h)\). Equality holds exactly when \(r\) is one of the minimizing alternatives. The proof is only the elementary fact that the minimum over a set cannot exceed the value at any member. This proves what a *specified fixed-denominator variant sensitivity score* does; it does not prove that every decrease is a meaningful recovery, that system rankings are preserved, or that every OIWER implementation is monotone in WER. The [Vimarsha author implementation](https://github.com/AI4Bharat/Vimarsha) defines \(N\) as the reference-word count in the *selected* alignment, so phrase split/merge alternatives can change the denominator. A real study must freeze and expose its chosen scorer, output exact edit counts, and retain ordinary WER as the prespecified anchor.

## What a defensible next experiment would have to add

Our [night-1 pilot](../../results/night1/RUN_REPORT.md) is an auditable baseline, not a new metric or model: it decodes the full 3,136-clip MUCS test and 5,176-clip HiACC pool with one Whisper checkpoint, finds a 15.02-point **post-QC** MUCS auto-versus-forced-Hindi WER difference, and supplies only provisional automatically mined script pairs and dictionary term candidates. The source paper's tutorial/speaker caveat means the MUCS resamples and frozen split must be described by **recording/tutorial cluster**, even though the source files name the field `utt2spk`. The pilot's original report incorrectly described these as verified speakers; the corrected report preserves the same point estimates and intervals while changing the interpretation. The original MUCS test transcripts also have documented alignment noise, so listening review is essential before counting model-specific term failures. [MUCS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.00235).

The clearest narrow question is **how an application-available entity vocabulary and language/script policy affect English term and name fidelity in real Hindi–English conversation, while keeping Hindi words and absent terms correct**. This is an *inference from the gaps above*, not an established novelty claim. Freeze a mixed-script reference convention and annotation for actual embedded-English spans, names, and semantic identity. On one appropriate source such as CoSHE-Eval, report original single-reference WER, a separately labeled human-approved variant score, embedded-span PIER or entity WER, entity precision/recall and false insertion, and non-entity WER. Evaluate monolingual controls and words with no listed target. Have bilingual reviewers listen to disputed transcript pairs; script counts, edit distance, and transliteration systems alone cannot certify what was spoken. Preserve source license and clip IDs. [MUCS](https://arxiv.org/html/2104.00235); [PIER](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09512); [OIWER](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00941); [CoSHE-Eval](https://huggingface.co/datasets/soketlabs/CoSHE-Eval).

For inference, hold audio/model/decoding fixed and compare no-context automatic detection, an explicitly specified language hint, a plain independently available entity list, the same entities in both scripts, and a candidate selector such as CB-Whisper or RECAST where implementation is feasible. Keep a reference-derived list as an explicitly labeled diagnostic. Inject plausible phonetic and irrelevant-domain distractors, then measure prompt-induced substitutions and hallucinations as well as target recovery. Freeze prompt choice on development recordings or verified speakers before test; stratify the paired uncertainty by verified speaker or original recording rather than treating segmented clips/words as independent. A prompt list containing the true term is not by itself an upper bound on performance: changing a generative model's context can also change unrelated words and EOS behavior, as demonstrated empirically by IndicContextEval. [IndicContextEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19157); [CB-Whisper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.262/); [RECAST](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.203/); [Whisper source](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/transcribe.py).

A deployed decoder-policy claim has a simple necessary and sufficient test. For item (i), let (e_i^A,e_i^F) be word-error counts under automatic and forced-language decoding, (g_i\in\{0,1\}) a gate fixed **without test references** that chooses forced decoding when (g_i=1), and (N=\sum_i |r_i|). Then

\[
\mathrm{WER}(A)-\mathrm{WER}(g)=\frac{\sum_i g_i(e_i^A-e_i^F)}{N}.
\]

This follows by substituting (e_i^g=(1-g_i)e_i^A+g_i e_i^F) into pooled WER and cancelling the automatic terms. Thus a gate improves pooled WER **if and only if** its chosen clips have positive total error advantage; observing that some clips are helped is insufficient. An oracle (g_i^*={\bf1}[e_i^F<e_i^A]) yields the best fixed-pair selection, but its use of reference errors makes it a diagnostic bound rather than a deployable method. This identity is methodological bookkeeping, not a proof that any trainable or heuristic gate generalizes; the latter requires a frozen development policy and untouched recording-heldout evaluation.

**Claims to avoid:** “first Hinglish benchmark”; “first transliteration-aware metric”; “first domain-entity prompt”; “first language-hint ablation”; “first ASR hallucination analysis”; “general fairness across Indian accents”; “orthographic variants prove semantic correctness.” The present repo's [core-study positioning](../bridge_open/core_study/LITERATURE_POSITIONING.md) already limits its six-recipe FLEURS/Svarah audit to an auditable quality/resource/failure comparison, and [its readiness note](../bridge_open/core_study/REVIEW_READINESS.md) says human meaning review is pending. An expanded Hinglish/entity claim therefore requires **new audio, reference annotations, or a rigorously distinct controlled analysis**, not relabeling existing high-WER tails.
