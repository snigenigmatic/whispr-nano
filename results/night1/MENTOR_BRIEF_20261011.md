# Capstone evidence brief for the 16 October review

**Team: C. Kaustubh et al. · Updated: 13 October 2026**

We have completed auditable public-data ASR baselines and one **frozen, speaker-disjoint inference-time control**. On 405 Lahaja clips from 106 speakers unseen during development, Whisper-small greedy decoding scored **119.73% WER**; a fixed word-loop repair scored **77.79%**, a paired reduction of **41.94 points [32.36, 52.14]**. A matched five-beam decoder scored **78.38% WER**; its 0.59-point difference from repair has a paired speaker interval of **[−5.84, 7.20]** and does not establish either method as better. Applying the *unchanged* frozen repair after five-beam decoding produced an **exploratory 64.87% WER**, 13.50 points [8.39, 19.18] below beam5. The combination was chosen after inspecting the held-out beam aggregate, so it requires a new untouched cohort before a confirmatory claim. All small-model scores remain poor, and the text-only repair is unsafe on genuine repetition. A stronger Whisper large-v3-turbo reference scored **39.09% WER** on the same clips. For the original Indian-English goal, two open models were independently accepted on the same **518 Svarah recordings** from one recovered shard. We also retain complete predictions for **3,136 MUCS Hindi–English lecture segments** and **5,176 HiACC adult/child Hinglish clips**. Model/data revisions, decode settings, hashes, predictions, and scores are recorded locally; only aggregate Lahaja receipts are shared. A [Hugging Face A100 replication](lahaja_beam5_hf_gpu_replication_20261013.json) of the 405-clip five-beam condition completed at **79.41% WER**, versus **78.38%** on local MPS with the same pinned data selection, model revision, decode settings, and denominator. The runs used different PyTorch versions and hardware. The cloud job did not export per-clip predictions, so this 1.04-point difference remains unexplained and the independently checked MPS predictions remain the source for paired claims. The [HF job](https://huggingface.co/jobs/snigenigmatic/6ace098a24c0e0c53d7cea30) and aggregate log hash are recorded in the receipt.

The additional [Lahaja benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ai4bharat/Lahaja) is now accessible to the team's HF account. Its four released Parquet shards contain **6,152 Hindi clips**, **132 speaker IDs**, **83 districts**, and **18 states**; durations total **12.32 hours**. The fields include speaker ID, native state/district/language, read versus extempore scenario, and verbatim transcript. This makes it a useful *Hindi regional robustness* check, not a Hinglish substitute. We score the `verbatim` field because 3,960 of 6,152 `normalized` rows contain brackets, including inserted glosses; the exact reference policy matters. A fixed first-shard Whisper-small greedy run produced long repetitions (12 of 104 clips had hypotheses at least three times the reference length; 27 clips had WER above 100%). CPU float32 and attention-mask controls reproduced the loops. Five-beam decoding improved the *development* WER from 120.63% to 79.47%, but still left 12 clips above 100% WER and 18 generation-cap hits. The zero-extra-model-compute loop repair scored 73.19% on those development clips before it was frozen for the held-out run. These diagnostics identify a decoder failure, not a clean model-capability comparison. Raw WER also counts spoken number words versus written digits as errors. Sixty-six of 405 held-out `verbatim` references contain bracketed annotation items such as `[uhh]`; a post hoc sensitivity that removes those items changes the paired reduction from 41.94 to 42.94 points, while the literal-reference result remains the headline.

**What this yields for the review:** a source-checked Svarah Indian-English comparison, reproducible MUCS and HiACC baselines, a measured MUCS decoder-policy failure, a sharply defined HiACC script-dependent error pattern, and a paired Lahaja small-model intervention on unseen speakers. The exploratory beam-plus-repair combination is promising but was selected after inspecting a held-out result and cannot be the paper's confirmatory estimate. The next defensible paper question is whether a compact open-ASR policy can suppress *unsupported* repetitions while preserving actually spoken repeats and task-relevant numbers and terms. A public [genuine-repetition benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Scicom-intl/Whisper-Hallucination) exposes the limitation of our text-only rule: applying it to perfect references would alter **960/1,440** examples and delete **6,480/9,120** words ([audited counterexample](lahaja_loop_repair_positive_control_20261011.json)). That is an oracle structural test, not an observed model false-positive rate, but it rules out presenting the rule as a safe general solution. A [development-only selective beam fallback](SELECTIVE_BEAM_DEV_20261013.md) also failed to beat full beam decoding or the text repair. The paper-grade next step is an acoustic-support gate, evaluated on both real decoder loops and genuinely repeated speech, plus an intervention on Indian-English/Hinglish data that matches the original capstone goal. Sarvam remains an optional comparator after written evaluation-use permission arrives.

The [original Lahaja paper](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2408.11440) already reports regional weaknesses and failures on named entities and specialized terms. Repeating those observations is a benchmark check, not a new contribution. The publication test must be a *measured intervention* on a small open model, with held-out speakers, term and number-specific accuracy, absent-term false positives, and overall WER reported together.

Nor is number-aware scoring alone new: [The Hidden Cost of Digits](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2609.21084) studies number normalization's effect on ASR WER, and [mu-bench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2609.32082) evaluates form-field transcription against human judgments. The narrow contribution to test here is a low-footprint correction or decoding policy for Indian-accented open ASR, plus evidence that it preserves the actual number or entity value on speakers unseen during policy development.
